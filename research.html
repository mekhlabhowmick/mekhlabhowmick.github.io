Research

My doctoral thesis, Rethinking Domestic Labour: A Theoretical Inquiry, re-theorises domestic labour through a class-focused approach where class is understood in terms of organization of surplus labour. I examine the class organisation of domestic labour in households along with its conditions of existence and explore how different forms of domestic production can constitute the domestic sphere.

Further, the study empirically investigates the outsourcing of domestic work to waged domestic workers, the organization of domestic and care work through domestic staffing agencies, and the production of commodities through home-based enterprises, both capitalist and non-capitalist. These empirical investigations serve to substantiate the theoretical argument that the organization of domestic labour in such varied kinds of domestic production point to possibilities of transition within Indian households.

    Original Bengali title: Charjar Darshan: Karl Marxer Theses on Feuerbach
    Saros Publishers, Kolkata · ISBN 9788199253308
    Summary

    In 1845, Marx articulated the initial framework of The German Ideology in the form of eleven “Theses on Feuerbach.” This book presents a Bengali translation of the Theses on Feuerbach, rendered with reference to both the German and English texts, accompanied by a discussion of each thesis.

    In the twenty-first century, at a time when conventional left-wing politics faces uncertainty regarding political philosophy, Marx’s “Philosophy of Praxis” offers a framework for thinking about a different kind of transformative politics.

  2. Domestic Labour and Class: Blurred Boundaries between Capitalist and Domestic Production
    Book Chapter · 2026
    Mekhla Bhowmick
    Original Bengali title: Grihashrom ebong Sreni: Pujitontrer Ghor o Bahir
    In The Iron Gates of Capital(ism): Marx’s Capital and Alternative Paths (ed.)
    Saros Publishers, Kolkata · ISBN 9788199253391
    Summary

    This chapter presents a critical reading of existing theories of domestic labour, arguing that the concept has, at best, remained under-theorised or only partially theorised, often being reduced to a particular aspect, system, event, or phenomenon.

    A class-focused analysis offers a radically different way of understanding the heterogeneous nature of domestic production, which in turn shapes the processes and forms of domestic labour.

Works in progress

  1. Surplus at Home: Rethinking Domestic Labour through Class
    Mekhla Bhowmick
    Summary Building on Fraad, Resnick and Wolff’s class analysis of the household, this paper examines domestic space through the analytical tools of class matrix and class set. It argues that domestic space may be constituted by multiple, overlapping class structures—both exploitative and non-exploitative—giving rise to multiple class positions held by domestic labour. It further challenges the conventional separation of the household from commodity production, showing that domestic production is diverse, decentralised, and disaggregated.
  2. Beyond the Factory Walls: Capital’s Reorganization of Domestic Labour
    Mekhla Bhowmick
    Summary Taking the case of domestic and care workers employed by households from staffing agencies, this paper argues that capitalism does not need to relocate all production physically into the household in order to shape domestic labour. Its dominance can be exercised precisely through the capacity to mediate and reorganize social reproduction, drawing domestic activities into wider circuits of commodification and accumulation. It reflects not only a change in the organization of domestic work, but also the growing penetration of capitalist class relations into spheres of life, until now historically viewed as external to capitalist production.
  3. Capital(ism) at the doorstep: Commodified Labour Power in Household
    Mekhla Bhowmick
    Summary This paper examines the household not as a space insulated from capitalist relations, but as a site in which capitalist social relations can penetrate. We deal with the case of outsourcing of domestic work, asking how the employment of paid domestic workers introduces the commodification of labour-power into a household where the immediate products of labour remain non-commodities produced for self-consumption. The study is substantiated with a primary survey of more than fifty domestic workers working in several households of North 24 Parganas and Kolkata districts of West Bengal.
