Approach

A class-focused reading of domestic labour and the household, in the tradition of Resnick and Wolff, grounded in Indian cases.

Doctoral research

My thesis, Rethinking Domestic Labour: A Theoretical Inquiry, re-theorises domestic labour through class. Where earlier theories reduce domestic labour to a single relation — to the economy, to patriarchy, or to social reproduction — I argue that a household can hold one or many class structures, and that domestic production is diverse rather than uniform.

My working papers develop this in several directions: the role of class and surplus inside the home; home-based enterprises in India that produce commodities for the market; paid domestic work and the entry of wage labour into the household; care work arranged through staffing agencies; and cases where households themselves become sites of capitalist production.
