Bio

I work on the theory of domestic labour. My doctoral thesis, Rethinking Domestic Labour: A Theoretical Inquiry, re-theorises domestic labour using a class-focused approach in the tradition of Resnick and Wolff.

Existing theories — Marxist feminist, radical feminist (dual-systems), and social reproduction theory — have placed domestic labour at the centre of debate. I argue that, because they share a deterministic method, they tend to reduce domestic labour to a single relation: to the capitalist economy, to patriarchy, or to social reproduction. I instead read domestic labour through class, and show that a household can hold one or many class structures, and that domestic production is diverse rather than uniform.

I study these questions mainly in Indian households — including home-based enterprises, paid domestic work, and care work arranged through agencies. Alongside my research, I translate academic work between Bengali and English, and have co-authored a Bengali book and a book chapter on Marx.
